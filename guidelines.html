Participation Guidelines

Everything a team needs to know, from registration to the final paper. Task-specific rules are below; the paper rules are on the paper submission page.

On this page Registration Teams Data and terms of use Tracks and allowed resources Phases and runs Submission format Leaderboard and ranking Papers FAQ Contact

1. Registration

  • Register your team through the registration form. One registration per team, listing the team name, all members, affiliations and a contact e-mail.
  • Registration is free and stays open until the test submissions close. Registering early means you receive the data-release announcement, the Slack invitation and any schedule changes.
  • Registration is also required to create your Codabench account for the competition and to receive the OpenReview submission link.

2. Teams

  • Anyone can participate, individually or as a team, at no cost: students, academics, industry and independent researchers.
  • A person may be a member of only one team in TariqQA. The same team may enter one or both tracks.
  • Team names appear on the public leaderboard and in the overview paper. Please keep them appropriate.
  • Organizers and their close collaborators may submit runs for reference, but these are marked as such and excluded from the official ranking.

3. Data and terms of use

  • The training and development sets (with labels) and the test set (without labels) are released from the official GitHub repository on the dates of the timeline.
  • Data licence: TBA. By downloading the data you agree to use it for research purposes within the terms of that licence and to cite the task overview paper in any publication that uses it.
  • Requesting the data commits your team to submit at least one official run. Teams that download the data and silently withdraw may be excluded from future editions.
  • The test set must not be annotated manually, used for training or model selection, or redistributed with labels. Gold labels for the test set are released after the evaluation phase.

In the closed-book track the model may only see the question and its three options at inference time. Anything the model knows must come from its parameters. If in doubt whether a technique counts as "retrieval", ask on Slack before the evaluation phase.

  • Each run declares its track at upload time. The last valid run uploaded before the deadline is the official run for that track, unless the team names another one on Slack before the deadline.
  • Runs uploaded outside the evaluation window are not official and cannot be reported as such in papers.
  • All deadlines are 23:59 anywhere on Earth (UTC-12).

6. Submission format

Upload a zip archive to Codabench. The archive must contain a single file named predictions.csv at its root, encoded in UTF-8, with a header row and one line per test question:

id,answer
TQA-00001,A
TQA-00002,C
TQA-00003,B
  • id is the question identifier from the test file; every test id must appear exactly once.
  • answer is exactly one of A, B or C. Missing, empty or invalid values are scored as wrong.
  • Row order does not matter. Extra columns are ignored.
  • Validate the file locally with the script in the repository before uploading. A sample file shows the format.

7. Leaderboard and ranking

  • Systems are ranked by accuracy on the test set, per track. Ties are broken by macro-F1 over the three labels.
  • Per-variety accuracy (MSA, Darija, English) and the split between driving-knowledge and article-grounded questions are reported for every official run.
  • Results are announced on the timeline date and summarised in the task overview paper, which also includes a short description of every system that submitted a paper.

8. Papers

  • Every team with an official run is invited to submit a system description paper (4 pages plus unlimited references and appendices) through OpenReview. Details are on the paper submission page.
  • Papers must cite the TariqQA overview paper and report the official run, clearly separated from any post-evaluation experiments.
  • At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present the work. Papers without a registered presenter by the early-registration deadline may be removed from the proceedings.
  • Participants act as reviewers for other system papers; every review is checked by an organizer.

9. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to register before downloading the data?

Yes. Registration is free and takes two minutes. It lets us keep teams informed and keep the leaderboard tidy.

Can I participate in only one track?

Yes. Enter the closed-book track, the open track, or both. Each track has its own leaderboard and run limit.

Can I use a proprietary API such as GPT, Claude or Gemini?

Yes, in both tracks, as long as the model is not given external documents or tools at inference time in the closed-book track, and as long as you document exactly which model and version you used.

May I translate the questions or generate extra training data?

Yes. Translation, paraphrasing and synthetic data generation are allowed in both tracks and must be described in the paper. Generating data from the test questions is not allowed.

Is the test set labelled by language variety?

The test file contains only the question and its options. Variety labels are released after the evaluation phase, together with the gold answers, so that teams can analyse their results.

How many official runs do I get?

Up to three per team and track during the evaluation phase. Development-phase runs are unlimited.

Do I need an OpenReview account?

Yes, every author needs one to submit and to review. System and overview papers are not anonymous, and teams may post their papers on arXiv.

Is there a participation fee?

No. Participation in the shared task is free. One author of each accepted paper registers for the AbjadNLP workshop to present the work.

Should I release my code?

It is strongly encouraged. Add the URL to your paper; replicability is one of the review criteria.

Are there ethical considerations I should discuss?

Yes. Describe potential misuse and limitations of your system, in particular the risk of giving wrong legal advice to drivers, in a short paragraph of the paper.
