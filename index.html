01

Overview of the Shared Task

نظرة عامة على المهمة المشتركة

TariQA (طريق, tariq: road) is a shared task on answering questions about Moroccan traffic law, organized as part of the AbjadNLP 2027 Workshop on natural language processing for languages that use the Arabic script. Given a question about driving rules, road signs, right of way, penalties, licence points, vehicle documents or the articles of Law 52-05 (the Moroccan Highway Code, Code de la Route), a system must choose the single correct answer among three candidates.

Questions are written the way Moroccans actually ask them: in Modern Standard Arabic and Moroccan Darija (often with French loanwords such as embrayage or compteur). The task combines dialectal understanding, precise country-specific legal knowledge, and robustness to distractors that are deliberately close to the correct answer.

The task is open to everyone: academic groups, industry teams and individual researchers. Participation is free, and every team is invited to submit a system description paper to the AbjadNLP 2027 proceedings.

At a glance

  • Task: three-option multiple-choice question answering (output A, B or C).
  • Domain: Moroccan Highway Code and driving-school theory.
  • Languages: Modern Standard Arabic and Moroccan Darija, in one shared test set.
  • Tracks: Closed-book (parametric knowledge only) and Open (retrieval, tools, anything documented).
  • Metrics: accuracy (primary), macro-F1 (secondary), per-variety breakdown.
  • Submission: predictions on Codabench, papers on OpenReview.
  • Cost: free to participate. One author of an accepted paper registers for the workshop.
02

Motivation

الدوافع

Road safety is a public concern

Knowing the Highway Code is the first line of defence on Moroccan roads. Every year hundreds of thousands of candidates prepare for the theory part of the driving licence, and millions of drivers have questions about fines, points and procedures. Reliable, accessible answers in the languages people actually speak are a matter of safety, not convenience.

A domain that punishes hallucination

Legal questions are factual and unforgiving: the right amount in dirhams, the right number of points, the right deadline, the right article. A fluent answer that is wrong is worse than no answer. The task is a clean testbed for faithfulness and precise knowledge in Arabic-script LLMs.

Darija is low-resource and code-mixed

Moroccan Darija has few benchmarks, and traffic talk is full of French and Spanish loanwords, Latin-script tokens and local idiom. Models that excel on Modern Standard Arabic routinely stumble on "واش خاصني نوقف عند علامة STOP؟". TariQA measures that gap on a concrete, useful task.

Applications are immediate

Driving-school tutors, citizen-facing legal assistants, chatbots for the licence theory exam, road-safety campaigns, and legal NLP for the broader Maghreb. Systems built for TariQA transfer directly to products people in Morocco would use tomorrow.

03

Data Collection and Creation

جمع البيانات وإنشاؤها

The TariQA dataset contains multiple-choice questions about Moroccan traffic law. Each question comes with three candidate answers (A, B, C) and exactly one gold label. Questions are drawn from two complementary sources:

  • Driving knowledge. Questions of the kind used in driving-school theory tests: road signs, right of way, overtaking, lights, speed, safe behaviour, vehicle documents and insurance.
  • The legal code. Questions grounded in specific articles of Law 52-05 and its implementing texts: classes of violations, fines, point deductions, prison terms, deadlines and administrative procedures. Article citations will be reported with the data release.

Distractors are plausible and lexically close to the correct option (same structure, different number, duration or authority), so surface-level matching is not enough. Label distribution will be reported with the MSA and Darija data release.

    Task Description

    وصف المهمة

    Input and output

    Input question A B C

    A question and its three candidate answers, in MSA or Darija.

    Output answer ∈ {A, B, C}

    Exactly one letter per question. Empty or invalid predictions count as wrong.

    Rules in brief

    • Any model, architecture or prompting strategy is allowed, in both tracks.
    • The test set must not be used for training, tuning or model selection, and must not be annotated by hand.
    • Teams enter one or both tracks and declare the track with each run.
    • Every resource used (models, corpora, tools, APIs) is reported in the system description paper.
    • Up to three official test runs per team and track. The last valid run is the official one.

    Tracks

    Track 1

    Closed-book

    Systems answer from what they already know. Fine-tuning on the official training data and any pre-trained model are allowed, but no external text may be consulted at inference time: no retrieval over the Highway Code, no web search, no tools. The track measures how much Moroccan traffic law a model has actually internalized.

    Track 2

    Open

    Anything goes, as long as it is documented: retrieval-augmented generation over Law 52-05 and its decrees, tool use, ensembles, proprietary APIs, additional training data, agents. The track measures the best achievable performance and encourages creative use of the legal sources.

    Evaluation

    MetricRoleNotes
    AccuracyPrimary, used for rankingShare of test questions whose predicted letter equals the gold letter.
    Macro-F1Secondary, tie-breakerComputed over the three labels A, B, C.
    Per-variety accuracyReportedMSA and Darija subsets, and driving-knowledge versus article-grounded questions.

    The official scorer will be released in the GitHub repository together with the training data, so that development scores are directly comparable with the leaderboard.

    Submission format

    Upload a zip archive to Codabench containing a single file named predictions.csv with one row per test question, in any order:

    id,answer
TQA-00001,A
